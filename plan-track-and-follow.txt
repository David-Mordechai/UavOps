# Wide-area tracking: every object has an ID, and any one can be followed

## Context
Today a search detection is a single sighting (`DetectionReport`, time of capture). After the
search nothing keeps watching, so a moving car is lost, and with several red cars nothing says
which is which.

**The operator's model (2026-09-30):** the payload looks down from 1,000 ft or more and sees a
wide picture. The onboard system gives **every object in it a unique ID** and keeps tracking each
one: its location, speed, heading and state. The operator sees them all, asks about any of them,
and can pick one for a UAV to follow.

This is how wide-area surveillance works (researched 2026-09-30; sources at the end):
- **Everything in view is tracked,** not one target. Each track has an id, position/velocity from a
  Kalman filter, a state (tentative / confirmed / coasting on prediction / lost) and last-seen.
  Tracks are sent with the video as MISB ST 0903 (VMTI).
- **Split by speed:**
  - a fast detector finds every vehicle in every frame (YOLO-class, ~30 fps on a Jetson);
  - a multi-object tracker keeps the IDs (ByteTrack / DeepSORT: motion gating plus appearance, so
    two red cars crossing don't swap IDs);
  - the slow vision model (ours: 2-5 s a frame) only describes tracks and answers questions about
    them. It never runs per frame.
- **The operator nominates.** Several look-alikes are separate tracks; the operator picks one to
  follow. The system never guesses.

**Physics sets how wide "wide" is.** With the current camera (40° HFOV, 1280 px), a 4.5 m car is
~26 px at 1,000 ft AGL, ~13 px at 2,000 ft and ~6 px at 4,000 ft. Detectors get unreliable
below ~10-15 px. Real wide-area systems carry very high-resolution sensors for exactly this
reason, so the simulator's wide-area mode uses a 4K sensor (3840 px): ~3x the pixels per car,
tracking across ~500-900 m from our altitudes. Zoomed close-ups stay available for the vision
model to identify a track.

Out of scope for v1: fusing two UAVs' tracks of the same object into one global ID (each UAV's
IDs are its own, and the map shows both), and handing custody from one UAV to another.

## Architecture
```
UAV payload (wide, 4K, ~2 fps in the sim) ──▶ onboard: fast detector (all vehicles) ──▶ MOT (IDs, Kalman, appearance)
                                                  │ on demand: close-up + vision model → "T-12: red sedan"
                                                  ▼
             track list per frame (VMTI-like): id, class, position, velocity, state, description, confidence
                                                  │ ChatHub.ReportTracks → McpMoav (generic relay)
                                                  ▼
McpMoav: the current tracks picture ── tools: ListTracks / DescribeTrack / FollowTrack / StopFollowing
                                                  ▲
Operator (chat, voice) ── "what's moving in ZoneA?", "follow T-12 with 998", "which of them is a red car?"
Page: every track on the map (ID, trail, velocity arrow) and in the 3D camera (box + ID)
```
The tracks live onboard, as on a real UAV. McpMoav holds the ground picture and all meaning, and
the host only relays (the domain-separation rule).

## 1. The wide-area sensor and the fast detector
- **Sensor.** A payload mode `Wide`: 4K frames (`Simulator:WideSensorWidth`, default 3840) at the
  UAV's zoom (default 1x, 40°), at ~2 fps of sim time, looking straight down or at a locked point.
  It's a new frame stream beside the survey frames. It renders at the frame's sim time
  (`World/GroundWorld`), so moving objects are where they are.
- **Fast detector: two interchangeable implementations** behind `IObjectDetector`:
  - **Simulated** (first): the simulator knows every object, so it projects each object in view
    into the frame with the camera model. A detection needs enough pixels (≥ 10 px, from the car's
    size and ground sample distance); its box gets pixel noise, a miss probability that grows as
    pixels shrink, and occasional false positives. That's honest about resolution limits with no
    model, and it runs in the simulator.
  - **Real** (later): a YOLO-class ONNX model in the onboard detector (ONNX Runtime, CPU or GPU),
    with a permissively licensed model (Apache-2.0 RT-DETR / YOLOX, not AGPL YOLOv8), trained or
    fine-tuned on aerial vehicles (VisDrone). The model choice is measured on our rendered frames,
    and it runs on the Jetson later (open issue #11).
- **Objects:** the scenario objects (and traffic, if it's turned back on: `TrafficDensity` > 0,
  now off). The picture shows exactly what's on the ground.

## 2. Multi-object tracking (`src/UavOps.Onboard.Detector`, new `Tracking/`)
- **`TrackManager`** per UAV. Each frame:
  1. Predict every track (constant-velocity Kalman in local metres).
  2. Associate detections, ByteTrack-style: high-confidence detections first, by gated Mahalanobis
     distance plus an appearance cost (colour histogram of the box). Then low-confidence
     detections to the tracks left over. Hungarian assignment.
  3. Unmatched detections start tentative tracks (confirmed after 3 hits). Unmatched tracks coast,
     then are lost after `LostAfterSeconds` (8).
- **IDs:** `T-<n>` per UAV (e.g. "998:T-12" when both UAVs matter), never reused in a session.
- **Descriptions** on demand or in the background: the vision model looks at a zoom close-up of a
  confirmed track ("red sedan", "white van"). This reuses today's close-up path, with no target
  given, as the structure search does. It's cached per track and refreshed if the appearance
  changes. That's how "which of them is a red car?" is answered without running the model per
  frame.
- **Search targets become queries over tracks:** during a search, a track whose description
  matches the target word by word (today's matcher) is reported as a detection, with its track
  ID. The existing detection messages keep working, now with an ID and motion.

## 3. The track picture on the ground (McpMoav)
- **Wire:**
  - Fleet → host: `ChatHub.ReportTracks(TrackBatch)`, per UAV, ~1 per second: id, class,
    position, speed, heading, state, description, confidence, last seen. It's forwarded unchanged
    to McpMoav (`HostHubContract.FleetEvents.Tracks`), like detections.
  - Host → fleet: `SetTrackingMode(tail, Wide|Off)`. The existing `SetTrackingMode` is the GDT
    antenna's, so this gets its own name, `SetSensorMode`. Also `FollowTrack(tail, trackId)` and
    `StopFollowing(tail)`, through every layer as StartMission was. `FleetContractDriftTests`
    covers them.
- **`TrackPicture`** (McpMoav): the current tracks per UAV, with short history. "T-12" and "track
  12" resolve to its current position for Navigate / PointPayload (as detection names do today).
- **Tools** (`ToolsConfig.yaml`, tool-description-review skill):
  - `ListTracks(zoneOrArea?, filter?)`: e.g. "what's moving in ZoneA?", "any red cars?". Compact
    rows, ID / class / description / motion / where; the model never gets raw coordinates it
    doesn't need.
  - `DescribeTrack(trackId)`: asks the vision model now, if not described yet.
  - `FollowTrack(tailNumber, trackId)`: the UAV follows that track (section 4). A description
    instead of an ID ("the red car") resolves to the matching tracks. More than one asks the
    operator, listing each with its motion, through the generic `ChatHub.RelayAskOperatorChoice`,
    never the model's guess.
  - `StopFollowing(tailNumber)`.
- **Messages** (fixed text, with voice short forms), only on changes:
  - "998 is following T-12 (red sedan), ~40 km/h NE".
  - "998 lost T-12 near …, searching where it should be".
  - "998 found T-12 again".
  - A new object of the search target's kind appearing in view (today's detection message, now
    with an ID).

  There's no message per track update. The model's history gets a compact picture on request
  (`ListTracks`), not a stream.

## 4. Following one track
- **Aircraft mode `Following` (`SimFleet`):**
  - It circles the track's predicted position: `LoiterAround` with a moving centre, radius from
    altitude.
  - The gimbal is locked on the track: `PayloadTarget` is updated each tick; the 3D view's `LOCK`.
  - Wide mode stays on, so the other objects keep their IDs around it.
  - The zoom only narrows if the track gets too small in the frame.
- **When the followed track coasts or is lost:**
  1. Fly to the prediction and look there.
  2. Reassociation by the tracker (motion + appearance) brings the same ID back. A vision-model
     check on a close-up confirms it's the same car before the ID is continued.
  3. After `ReacquireSeconds` (60), report it lost and orbit the last prediction.

## 5. The page
- **Map:** every confirmed track as a small labelled symbol: ID, a velocity arrow, a short trail,
  coloured by state. The followed one is highlighted, with its predicted-position ring. Tracks
  from two UAVs of the same object show both IDs (no fusion in v1).
- **3D camera:** a box and ID on each tracked object (`T-12 · 40 km/h`), and the HUD shows the
  followed track. Boxes are drawn from the track list, geo-positioned, so they stay on the
  objects.
- **A tracks panel** in the sidebar: the list, filterable. Clicking one zooms to it.

## 6. Measuring it (the simulator knows the truth)
- **`TrackEvaluator`**, dev only, per run, at `GET /api/tracking/metrics`:
  - detection recall vs pixels-per-object (at 1,000 / 2,000 / 4,000 ft);
  - identity switches (a track moving onto another object);
  - track fragmentation (one object, several IDs);
  - position error;
  - custody % for a followed track;
  - reacquisition time.
- **Scenarios:** two red cars on the same road (crossing, overtaking), a car stopping under
  trees, and several parked look-alikes. Traffic can be switched on to load-test the tracker
  (thousands of objects).
- **Unit tests:**
  - Kalman predict/update.
  - Association: two cars crossing keep their IDs; a missed detection coasts.
  - Tentative → confirmed → lost.
  - The simulated detector's pixel-size threshold.
  - `FollowTrack` asks when a description matches two tracks.
  - Contract drift.
- **Live tests** (8 repeats):
  - "what's moving in ZoneA?" → `ListTracks`.
  - "follow T-12 with 998" → `FollowTrack`.
  - "follow the red car" with two red cars → the operator is asked.
  - "stop following" → `StopFollowing`.
- **End to end in Chrome:**
  1. Wide mode over ZoneA with two red cars and the parked vehicles.
  2. The IDs on the map and in the camera.
  3. "which of them is a red car?"
  4. "follow T-12 with 998", then watch it hold the car through the bends and under the trees.
  5. Report the metrics.

## Order
1. Wide sensor + simulated fast detector + MOT with IDs + the track list to McpMoav and the map
   (sections 1-3, 5). The operator already sees every object with an ID.
2. `ListTracks` / `DescribeTrack` and descriptions by the vision model; search targets as
   queries over tracks.
3. `FollowTrack` and the following mode, custody messages, reacquisition (section 4).
4. Metrics and scenarios (section 6). Then the real ONNX detector in the onboard service,
   compared against the simulated one on the same frames.

## Open questions
1. **Follow geometry:** circle the car (fixed-wing norm, what our UAVs do) or trail behind it?
   The plan assumes circling.
2. **One global ID across UAVs** (fuse 997's and 998's tracks of the same car) in v1, or per-UAV
   IDs first? The plan assumes per-UAV first.
3. **Real detector now or later:** the plan starts with the simulated detector (it models the
   pixel limits honestly and needs no model) and adds the ONNX one in step 4.
